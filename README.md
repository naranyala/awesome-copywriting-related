## Start Here — What Is Copywriting?

If you read only 5 pages, read these:

- [Copyhackers — What is Copywriting?](https://copyhackers.com/101/what-is-copywriting/) — best plain-English primer.
- [Neuroscience of Copywriting — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/) — how users actually read on the web.
- [Sleight of Hand vs. Substance — Joanna Wiebe](https://copyhackers.com/) — why great copy = research + clarity, not tricks.
- [The Boron Letters (free PDF)](https://www.garyhalbertletter.com/boron-letters/) — Gary Halbert's letters to his son. Still the best free intro to direct response thinking.
- [Scientific Advertising by Claude Hopkins (free)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33264) — 1923, still relevant. Short, test-everything mindset.

**Copywriting vs. adjacent crafts:**

- `Copywriting` → drives action (ads, landing pages, emails, sales pages, CTAs).
- `Content writing` → informs / entertains / ranks (blogs, guides, docs).
- `UX writing / microcopy` → guides action inside product (buttons, empty states, errors).
- `Technical writing` → explains complex things clearly (docs, white papers).
- `Brand copy / tone of voice` → makes a company sound like itself everywhere.

Pros do all five, but get paid for the first.

## Glossary — Talk Like a Pro

Terms you'll hear on day one of a client call. Learn them before you bill for them.

**Client / business side:**

- **Offer** — the full deal: product + price + guarantee + bonuses. A strong offer makes weak copy work; a weak offer makes great copy fail.
- **USP** — Unique Selling Proposition. The one reason to buy from you and not the next tab.
- **Positioning** — the category you occupy in the customer's head (April Dunford's definition: who you compete with, why you're different, what you win).
- **AVP** — Advertising Value Proposition. Positioning, but in one sentence a prospect would repeat to a friend.
- **Voice / Tone** — Voice = fixed personality (who the brand is). Tone = how that voice flexes by context (error message vs. launch email).
- **ICP** — Ideal Customer Profile. Who the *company* sells to (B2B). The buyer persona is the individual human.
- **Persona** — a fictional composite of the buyer: pains, goals, objections, vocabulary.
- **Buyer's journey** — awareness → consideration → decision (TOFU/MOFU/BOFU). Copy changes at every stage.
- **Pain points** — the specific problems keeping your reader up at night. Pains > features, always.
- **Desired outcome** — what the buyer actually wants (jobs-to-be-done), not what you sell.
- **Objections** — reasons not to buy. Price, trust, effort, timing, "not now". Answer all of them before the final CTA.

**Copy mechanics:**

- **Lead** — the first 1-2 lines. Its only job: keep them reading. (See: open loops, cold opens.)
- **Hook** — the angle or curiosity gap that stops the scroll. Different from the lead, which develops it.
- **Body copy** — everything between the hook and the CTA: argument, proof, story, objection handling.
- **CTA** — Call to Action. One per screen, verb-first ("Start my free trial", not "Submit").
- **Feature → Benefit → Meaning** — "256-bit encryption" (feature) → "your data stays safe" (benefit) → "you sleep at night" (meaning). Always land on meaning.
- **Bulleted swipe** — scannable bullets under a header. The workhorse of landing pages.
- **Proof stack** — testimonials, numbers, logos, case studies, demos, guarantees, press. More proof = less risk.
- **Risk reversal** — anything that moves risk off the buyer: free trial, money-back, cancel anytime.
- **P.S.** — the most-read line of a sales email after the subject line. Never waste it.
- **Open loop** — a promise of payoff to come ("I'll show you the 3-part trick at the end"). Pay it off or you lose trust.
- **Cliffhanger / curiosity gap** — the space between what they know and what they want to know.

**Measurement:**

- **CTR** — click-through rate (clicks ÷ impressions).
- **CVR / Conversion rate** — conversions ÷ visitors.
- **AOV** — average order value.
- **LTV / CAC** — lifetime value vs. customer acquisition cost. If LTV < CAC, no copy will save the offer.
- **A/B test** — split traffic between two variants. Change ONE variable.
- **Lift** — % improvement of variant over control. Never ship a "winner" without statistical significance.
- **Funnel** — the full path from first touch to purchase (ad → landing → cart → checkout → email).
- **Segmentation** — splitting a list by behavior/demographics so the message matches the person.
- **Deliverability** — whether your email actually lands in the inbox (not spam).
- **Open rate** — less reliable since Apple MPP; treat as directional only.

**Copywriting industry:**

- **Swipe file** — your personal collection of proven ads, emails, and pages.
- **Spec work** — sample work you write without a client, labeled "SPEC" in your portfolio.
- **Kill fee** — the % you're paid if the client cancels mid-project (standard: 50%).
- **Retainer** — a recurring monthly fee for reserved capacity/ongoing work.
- **Usage rights** — where and how long the client may run your copy. Separate from the IP itself.
- **Revision vs. rewrite** — a revision adjusts within the approved angle; a new angle is a new project. Say this in your contract.
- **Brief** — the document that tells you what to write, for whom, and why. No brief = no work.
- **White-label** — you write under the agency's/brand's name. Common, fine for volume, bad for portfolio.

## Types of Copywriting / Specializations

Pick one to start, add a second after you have proof. Specialists earn 20-40% more than generalists.

- **Direct Response** — sales pages, VSLs, magalogs, fundraising. Measured in revenue. Highest ceiling.
- **Email Copy** — newsletters, autoresponders, launches, lifecycle / CRM.
- **Website / Conversion Copy** — homepages, landing pages, product pages, about pages.
- **B2B / SaaS** — white papers, case studies, one-pagers, sales enablement. High, stable demand.
- **SEO Copywriting** — briefs + articles that rank *and* convert.
- **Ad Copy (Paid Social / PPC)** — Meta, Google, TikTok, LinkedIn. Short, iterative, data-driven.
- **E-commerce / Product Copy** — PDPs, Amazon listings, packaging, naming.
- **UX / Product Copy** — onboarding, microcopy, empty states. Works with designers/PMs.
- **Brand / Creative Advertising** — slogans, campaigns, scripts, OOH. Agency world.
- **Ghostwriting / Thought Leadership** — LinkedIn, X, books for founders/execs.
- **Fundraising / Nonprofit** — appeals, impact stories.
- **Financial / Health / Legal** — regulated niches. Harder entry, premium rates.

## Foundations — Frameworks, Formulas & Psychology

### Classic frameworks — learn these first

- **AIDA** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Oldest, still useful for structure.
- **PAS** — Problem, Agitate, Solve. Best for pain-driven offers.
- **BAB** — Before, After, Bridge. Great for transformations / SaaS.
- **4Ps (Halbert)** — Picture, Promise, Prove, Push.
- **4Cs** — Clear, Concise, Compelling, Credible. Your editing filter.
- **ACCA** — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action.
- **SLAP** — Stop, Look, Act, Purchase (for short ad copy).
- **StoryBrand (Donald Miller)** — Customer = hero, brand = guide. Plan → Success / Failure.
- **Jobs-To-Be-Done (JTBD)** — People don't buy products, they hire them for progress.

### Conversion frameworks (web-specific)

- **Copyhackers Hierarchy** — Research → Hypothesis → Copy → Test.
- **MECLABS Conversion Heuristic** — `C = 4m + 3v + 2(i-f) - 2a` (motivation, value prop, incentive/friction, anxiety).
- **LIFT Model (WiderFunnel)** — Value prop, relevance, clarity, urgency, distraction, anxiety.
- **Landing Page Hot Zones** — Hero (value prop + CTA above fold) → social proof → how it works → objection handling → risk reversal → final CTA.

### Persuasion psychology — the non-negotiables

- **Cialdini's 7 Principles** — Reciprocity, Commitment/Consistency, Social Proof, Authority, Liking, Scarcity, Unity. Start with *Influence* + *Pre-Suasion*.
- **Loss Aversion & Prospect Theory (Kahneman)** — Losses hurt ~2x more than gains feel good. Frame accordingly.
- **Cognitive Fluency** — Easy to read = true / trustworthy. Short sentences, concrete nouns.
- **Specificity > Superlatives** — "Helped 3,412 SaaS teams cut churn 18% in 90 days" beats "world-class solution".
- **Risk Reversal** — Guarantees, trials, clear refunds crush anxiety.
- **Voice of Customer (VoC)** — Use customer's exact words for pains, dreams, objections. Never invent language.

Further reading: *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Kahneman), *Predictably Irrational* (Ariely), *Nudge* (Thaler & Sunstein), *100 Things Every Designer Needs to Know About People* (Weinschenk).

## Templates, Briefs & Checklists

Copy/paste starters — adapt, don't fill blindly.

**Creative brief (must-have before any draft):**

```
1. Offer (1 sentence) + price + guarantee
2. Audience (who, JTBD, awareness stage 1-5)
3. Pains / dreams in THEIR words (3-5 verbatims)
4. Proof (numbers, testimonials, logos, demo)
5. Objections + answers (price, trust, effort, timing)
6. CTA (one!) + next step + friction list
7. Voice (3 adjectives + do/don't examples)
8. Constraints (length, SEO keywords, legal, deadline)
9. Success metric (CTR, CVR, revenue, booked calls)
```

**Pre-publish checklist:**

- [ ] One reader, one idea, one action?
- [ ] Headline promises specific outcome (not cleverness)?
- [ ] Lead mirrors VoC pain in < 30 words?
- [ ] Proof within first screen? (numbers > adjectives)
- [ ] Objections answered before CTA?
- [ ] CTA verb + value + urgency? (not "Submit")
- [ ] Grade 6-8 readable? (Hemingway)
- [ ] Scannable? (subheads, bullets, bold one idea per block)
- [ ] Mobile hero fits without scroll fatigue?
- [ ] Title < 60 chars, meta < 155, one H1?
- [ ] Links + forms tested? Analytics tagged?

## Exercises, Drills & 30-Day Challenge

Skill comes from reps, not reading. Do one drill daily (15-30 min).

**Warm-up drills:**

1. **Headline rewrite** — take 5 headlines you saw today, write 10 alternatives each. Score them against your top 3.
2. **Hand-copying** — literally type out a winning sales page or email by hand. You absorb rhythm, structure, and objection order faster than reading it.
3. **Feature → Meaning** — take 10 product features, write benefit + meaning for each.
4. **VoC mining** — open Reddit/Amazon reviews for a product, pull 10 verbatim pains, write a headline from each.
5. **One-line clarity** — describe a complex product in 7 words, then 5, then 3.
6. **Deconstruction** — read an ad/email, label every part: hook, proof, objection handling, risk reversal, CTA.
7. **Blind rewrite** — find a page with weak copy, rewrite it before reading theirs; compare.
8. **Freeze-frame cold open** — write 10 first lines for the same email. Only the first sentence matters.

**Weekly projects (build portfolio while learning):**

- Week 1: Landing page for a local business you love (spec).
- Week 2: 5-email welcome sequence for a SaaS (spec).
- Week 3: 10-ad set for Meta (one hook, 5 body variants).
- Week 4: Product page + Amazon listing for a real physical product.

**30-day challenge:**

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| 1-5 | Read Adweek Handbook ch. 1-10; hand-copy 1 winner/day |
| 6-10 | Learn PAS/AIDA/BAB; rewrite 5 landing pages each day |
| 11-15 | VoC research: interview 3 people or mine 100 reviews; write pain bank |
| 16-20 | Build your first full spec (landing page + email) with brief |
| 21-25 | Learn your niche tool (ESP / Surfer / Meta Ads Library); ship 2 more specs |
| 26-28 | Assemble portfolio (3-6 pieces); get 1 critique from a community |
| 29-30 | Write offer + rate card; send 10 outreach messages; book 1 discovery call |

Post your progress publicly (LinkedIn/X) — accountability compounds and doubles as marketing.

## Metrics — What to Measure & Report

Copy without measurement is opinion. Learn the numbers clients care about; report them in their language (revenue, not "engagement").

**Core metrics by asset:**

| Asset | Primary metric | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page | Conversion rate (CVR) | Bounce, scroll depth, time on page |
| Email | Revenue per send / CTR | Open rate (directional), unsubscribes, spam |
| Ad copy | CTR → CPC → CPA | Hook rate (3-sec views), frequency |
| Homepage | Assisted conversions, demo bookings | Scroll, CTA CTR |
| PDP / product page | Add-to-cart rate, CVR | Reviews read, return rate |
| Cold outreach | Reply rate, meetings booked | Positive reply % |
| SEO article | Organic clicks, conversions | Rankings, dwell time, AI citations |

**How to run a test:**

1. Hypothesis: "Changing X will improve Y because Z (VoC quote / data)."
2. One variable (headline, offer, CTA, proof order).
3. Baseline → variant → significance (95%+ / sample size calculators).
4. Log every test: date, change, result, learning. This doc is your career moat.

**Reporting template (send monthly):**

```
[Asset] — [Period]
Baseline: X% → Result: Y% (+Z%)
Hypothesis:
What we changed:
Why it worked / didn't:
Next test:
```

Tools: GA4, PostHog, Clarity (free heatmaps), your ESP dashboard, Meta Ads Manager. If you can tie a headline change to dollars, you will never be the cheapest option again.

## How to Become a Professional Copywriter

### 0. Mindset shift

You are not paid to write. You are paid to **think** (research, positioning, offer) then make thinking legible. Best copywriters spend 70% researching, 20% drafting, 10% editing. Charge for thinking.

### 1. Skills roadmap (0 → hireable in 90 days)

**Days 1-30 — Foundations:**

1. Read *Adweek Handbook* + *Everybody Writes* + Boron Letters.
2. Hand-copy 5 winners (sales page, email sequence, ad, landing page, PDP).
3. Learn AIDA/PAS/BAB + Cialdini + Schwartz awareness stages (can explain to a 12-year-old).
4. Write daily 300 words: rewrite boring headlines, product descriptions, CTAs you see.
5. Grammar baseline: Grammarly + Hemingway on everything.

**Days 31-60 — Specialize + prove:**

1. Pick ONE lane (email, website, SEO, ads, B2B SaaS).
2. Do 2-3 free/spec projects for real businesses (nonprofit, friend's startup) with brief + metric.
3. Learn its tool: ESP for email, Surfer/Ahrefs for SEO, Meta Ads Library for ads.
4. Publish 2 breakdowns (LinkedIn / blog): "Before → After → Why". This IS your portfolio.
5. Get feedback: Copy Chief / Copywriter Club / one paid critique. Iterate.

**Days 61-90 — Get paid:**

1. Portfolio: 3-6 spec + real pieces with context (goal, audience, process, result/mock result). Copyfolio/Contra/Framer.
2. Offer one packaged service (e.g., "5-email welcome sequence in 7 days — $1,000" or "SaaS landing page rewrite — $1,500").
3. Outreach 5/day: warm network + LinkedIn + niche Slack + job boards. Personalized Loom > 100 cold DMs.
4. First 3 clients: over-deliver, ask for testimonial + referral + numbers. Raise price every 2 clients.
5. Systems: contract, 50% upfront, 2 revisions, final payment = IP transfer.

What separates pros: research docs, briefs, revision rationale ("changed X because Y, expect Z"), measurement, deadlines kept.

### 2. Portfolio that wins clients

Structure each piece like a mini case study:

> **Title + role** (e.g., Email sequence — strategy + copy)
> **Context:** business, audience, goal, constraints
> **Process:** 3 bullets (VoC quotes, angle chosen, framework)
> **Result:** metric or learning ("+22% CTR in test" / "spec — hypothesis: ...")
> **Assets:** live link + Google Doc with comments + Loom walkthrough

No clients? Do specs, but label them `SPEC`. Rewrite a bad landing page, a boring Amazon listing, a nonprofit appeal. Quality of thinking > logos.

### 3. Niching (when + how)

Stay generalist for first 3-5 paid projects. Then niche by:

- **Industry you understand** (e.g., you were a nurse → health SaaS; dev → devtools),
- **Problem you solve** (abandoned carts, demo bookings, onboarding activation),
- **Channel you love** (email, landing pages, Amazon).

Test: "I help [who] get [outcome] with [deliverable] without [pain]" — if you can name 50 dream clients, it's viable.

### 4. Finding clients (what actually works in 2026)

1. **Warm + proof posts** — weekly LinkedIn teardown + offer. Highest ROI for beginners.
2. **Direct outreach to SaaS/agencies** — 2-sentence + 90-sec Loom rewriting their hero. 10-20% reply if specific.
3. **Marketplaces to start, not to stay** — Contently, ClearVoice, Contra, Upwork/Fiverr Pro for reps, then move off.
4. **Agencies/white-label** — email studios, webflow shops always need copy. Retainer-friendly.
5. **Referrals/partnerships** — designers, devs, media buyers, SEO folks. Give 10-15% finder's fee.
6. **Job boards** (below) — filter for contract + "direct response / lifecycle / CRO".

Avoid: content mills (<$0.10/word), free tests > 300 words, "exposure", rev-share only.

### 5. Pricing — 2026 US benchmarks

Sources: AWAI 2026 Pricing Guide (80 projects), EFA 2026 Rate Chart (1,100+ members), SoloPricing / Freelance Writers Den 2025 surveys. Use as anchors, calculate your floor.

| Deliverable | Junior (0-2y) | Mid (3-5y) | Senior / Specialist |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blog post 800-1500w | $150-$300 | $300-$600 | $600-$1,500 |
| Single email | $75-$175 | $175-$400 | $400-$1,000+ |
| Email sequence 5-7 | $500-$1,000 | $1,000-$2,500 | $2,500-$6,000 |
| Landing page | $300-$750 | $750-$2,000 | $2,000-$5,000 |
| Website 5-7 pages | $1,500-$3,000 | $3,000-$7,000 | $7,000-$15,000 |
| White paper 2.5-5k w | $1,000-$2,500 | $2,500-$5,000 | $5,000-$10,000 |
| Social (monthly) | $500-$1,000 | $1,000-$2,500 | $2,500-$5,000 |
| Hourly | $50-$85 | $85-$160 | $160-$300+ |
| Retainer | $1,000-$2,500 | $2,500-$5,000 | $5,000-$12,000+ |

EFA median: $60-$126/hr, 25-45¢/word blogs, $1+/word medical/scientific/technical. B2B SaaS/fintech/health +20-40%. UK avg ~£480/day (£250 junior → £800+ senior/specialist £2k).

Pricing rules:

- Project > hourly (price value + thinking, not time).
- 50% upfront, balance on draft delivery. Late fee 1.5%/mo.
- 2 revision rounds included, then hourly. Define "revision" vs "rewrite/new angle".
- Retainer = 10-15% discount for predictability, 30-day out, pause clause.
- Raise every 2-3 projects or when booked 3 weeks out. Never lower to win — reduce scope instead.
- Track effective $/hr. If <$80/hr mid-level, scope or price is wrong.

Full tables: [AWAI 2026 Pricing Guide (PDF)](https://www.professionalwritersalliance.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/rep_AWAI_Copywriting_Pricing_Guide.pdf), [EFA Rate Chart](https://www.the-efa.org/rates/), [SoloPricing 2026](https://www.solopricing.com/freelance-copywriter-rates-2026).

### 6. Proposals, contracts & process

Winning proposal = 1 page: diagnosis (their words) → angle/hypothesis → deliverables + timeline → price (2-3 tiers) → guarantee/next step. Attach 1 relevant sample + 1 testimonial.

Contract must-haves: scope, timeline, payment schedule, revisions, kill fee 50%, IP on final payment, usage rights, confidentiality, AI policy, late fees. Start from [Freelancers Union template](https://www.freelancersunion.org/) + lawyer review once earning.

Delivery process pros use: kickoff (30min) → research doc → outline/angle approval → draft in Google Docs (Suggest) → Loom walkthrough → 2 revision rounds → final + style notes + measurement plan → testimonial/referral ask.

### Client Communication Scripts

Copy-paste templates. Customize before sending — generic templates read as spam.

**Cold outreach (LinkedIn/email) — keep it 3 sentences + 1 CTA:**

```
Subject: [Their brand] homepage — quick idea

Hey [Name] — saw [specific thing, e.g., your hero says "streamline workflows"].
That phrase gets used by 40+ competitors; a more specific angle could be
[one-line idea, e.g., "cutting billing reconciliation from 3 days to 20 minutes"].

Wrote a 60-sec Loom of the rewrite if you want it: [link]. Worth a look?
```

**Discovery call questions (30 min, do 80% listening):**

1. What does success look like in 90 days (number)?
2. Who is your best customer? What did they say when they bought?
3. What do people say right before they churn / say no?
4. What's the #1 objection on sales calls?
5. What have you tried that didn't work?
6. What does your current copy get wrong about the customer?
7. If budget weren't a factor, what would you rewrite first?
8. Who else is on the team / who approves copy?

**Scope confirmation (before invoice):**

```
Hi [Name] — excited to start. Confirming scope:
Deliverables: [list]
Timeline: [dates + milestones]
Your inputs needed by [date]: [VoC quotes, logos, data, approvals]
Revisions included: 2 rounds on the approved angle
Total: $X — 50% to book, 50% on final delivery
Reply "confirmed" and I'll send the contract + invoice.
```

**Revision pushback (when scope creeps):**

```
Happy to make those changes — the original scope covered [X].
[The new request] is a new angle / new asset, so I'll send a quick
change order for $Y. Want me to proceed?
```

**Raising your rate (existing client, 3+ months in):**

```
Hi [Name] — I've loved working on [project]. As of [date], my rate
for [deliverable] moves from $A to $B (still 10% under my new-client
rate of $C). Your current project at $A is unaffected. Want to lock
in a retainer at the old rate for 3 months?
```

**Testimonial ask (after a win):**

```
Congrats on [result]! Two quick asks:
1) Mind sharing 2-3 lines on what it was like working together?
   (A sentence on the before/after helps most.)
2) Know anyone else who needs [your service]? I have 1 spot open this month.
```

### Running Your Copywriting Business

You're a one-person company. Treat it like one from day one.

**Legal & admin (do once, correctly):**

- Register an entity when it makes tax sense (LLC/S-corp US, Ltd UK, PT Indonesia). Talk to an accountant once you clear ~$30-50k/yr.
- Business bank account + separate savings for taxes (25-30% of every payment, US self-employment is ~15.3% on top of income tax).
- Standard contract reviewed by a lawyer once — reuse forever. Non-negotiable clauses: scope, kill fee 50%, IP transfer on final payment, AI disclosure policy, revision limits.
- Invoicing net-14 or net-30. Late fee 1.5%/mo. Chase on day 1 of overdue — politely, immediately.
- Bookkeeping from day 1 (Wave free, QuickBooks, Xero). Your future accountant will thank you.

**Pricing discipline (see full table above):**

- Calculate your floor rate first: `(annual income goal + taxes + tools + health + overhead) ÷ billable hours (1,000-1,200/yr)`.
- Track effective hourly on every project. If it drops below floor, scope or price is wrong.
- Never discount to win — reduce scope, extend timeline, or walk.

**Client management:**

- One point of contact. Multiple approvers = delayed projects.
- Written briefs before drafts. No brief, no work — even for friends.
- Async by default (Google Doc comments + Loom). Save calls for decisions.
- Set communication windows ("I reply within 1 business day"). Protect deep work.
- 50% upfront to book, balance on final. No exceptions.
- End every project with: final files + style notes + measurement plan + testimonial/referral ask + a "what's next" upsell.

**Systems that save you:**

- Proposal template, contract template, brief template, delivery checklist — one Notion page each.
- Swipe file (your own vault + client-approved pieces you can reuse structurally).
- CRM lite: a spreadsheet with `Name / Company / Stage / Last touch / Next action` beats any tool for the first 50 clients.
- Time tracking (Toggl) for 3 months to calibrate estimates.

**Taxes & money (US baseline; adjust for your country):**

- Quarterly estimated taxes if US (Form 1040-ES). Indonesia: PPh Final 0.5-2% for some freelancers, PPh 21 above thresholds — consult a local consultant.
- Health insurance budget line ($450-800/mo US).
- Emergency fund: 3-6 months expenses before going full-time.

### 7. Career paths

- **Freelance** — freedom, ceiling $100k-$300k+ with retainers/royalties. You = sales + delivery + admin.
- **In-house (SaaS/ecom)** — $65k-$140k+ US, stability, deep product knowledge, equity. Best to learn systems.
- **Agency/creative** — $55k-$120k+, varied briefs, mentorship, portfolio speed. Long hours at top shops.
- **Fractional / consultant** — audits + strategy + team coaching. Needs proof. $5k-$15k/mo per client.
- **Creator/educator** — courses, newsletter, templates. Leverage after proof, not before.

All paths reward the same: research depth, clarity, reliability, numbers.

### 8. AI-proofing yourself (2026 reality)

AI writes average fast. Clients pay for: insights (interviews, data), taste (angle, voice), accountability (testing, revenue). Workflow that wins:

`You (research + angle + VoC) → AI (10 rough angles/drafts) → You (rewrite in client voice + proof + objection handling) → Test → Learn doc`

Disclose AI use in contract, never paste confidential client data into public models without permission, keep sources, fact-check stats. Original interviews + product usage beat prompts every time.

## Ethics, Legal & AI Disclosure

- Don't fabricate testimonials, stats, before/afters. Ever.
- Substantiate health/finance claims (FTC / ASA / FDA rules). Get legal review for regulated niches.
- Disclose material connections (FTC Endorsement Guides). Mark ads/sponsored.
- Contracts: usage/licensing, moral rights waivers where needed, GDPR/CCPA for email lists (consent, unsubscribe, address).
- AI: disclose if client asks or if deliverable trains models; opt out of training where possible; never upload PII/client IP to public tools without written OK.
- Plagiarism: run Copyscape/Originality on final; keep research log to prove provenance.
- Accessibility: plain language, alt text, 4.5:1 contrast for CTAs, keyboard-navigable forms — ethical and converts better.

Useful: [FTC .com Disclosures](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-trade-commission-advertising-guides), [Freelancers Union contract resources](https://www.freelancersunion.org/), [Kath Pay / Holistic Email on consent].
